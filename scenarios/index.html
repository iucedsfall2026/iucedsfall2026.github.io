Scenarios

Two calculators that show how much an answer depends on its assumptions. Move the inputs and watch the threshold change.

These are illustrations of method. Inputs marked reference come from published benchmarks; inputs marked illustrative were invented to demonstrate the calculation. Neither calculator ranks or recommends a project.

Battery storage break-even, Cherokee Nation

What share of its upfront cost would a battery storage system have to return every year, through savings or avoided losses, just to pay for itself? And how much more would a distributed design (several small batteries instead of one central one) need to return?

reference 15 years in NREL's 2025 Annual Technology Baseline. Capped at 15 years, the reference life.
illustrative 7% default. Not a financing quote or a community preference.
reference 4% of capital per year in the 2025 Annual Technology Baseline.
illustrative 20% default. Not a quote or observed premium.

Break-even share by project life

Annual break-even share = capital recovery factor (life, rate) + fixed O&M share. Same formula as the project's pipeline.

What this leaves out
  • Charging costs, round-trip losses, dispatch rules, replacement, interconnection, taxes and incentives.
  • Any measured outage, load or tariff data for a real facility. Those require an authorized facility review.
  • Whether a central and a distributed system deliver the same service. The comparison assumes they do.

Break-even rehabilitation cost by comparison period

Break-even cost = (assistance per year − upkeep per year) × annuity factor (period, rate). Same formula as the project's pipeline.

What this leaves out
  • Real bids, budgets, eligible program costs and property condition. None were available to this project.
  • Whether the household, location, tenure and quality of housing are actually equivalent between the two options.
  • For scale, the HUD 2025 total development cost ceilings recorded in the pipeline run from $438,741 (1 bedroom) to $644,506 (5 bedrooms). These are regulatory limits, not construction costs.
